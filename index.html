jar·vis·ing

/ˈdʒɑː.vɪ.sɪŋ/ verb (present participle of jarvis); also noun New entry

  1. The act of creating innovative projects at scale.

    Building many things, quickly and in the open, and letting the ones that work stand on their own.

    "She spent the summer jarvising: eleven prototypes, three of them still running."

  2. Giving people the tools to do it themselves.

    The making is not kept but handed on, so that the next person starts further along than you did.

    "Proper jarvising ends with someone else doing it without you."

Origin
Early 21st century: from Jarvis, the name given to a home-built assistant that grew into a fleet of small, cooperating agents. The ‑ing marks that the work is never quite finished.
Usage
Not to be confused with tinkering, which has no ambition of scale, or shipping, which has no obligation to teach.
