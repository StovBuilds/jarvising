## Corrections applied (suggested wording)

1. **Bulbous bow (6b).** Replace "two in the bulbous bow that was later removed" with: "two in a bulbous bow casing, which was cut away on a couple of boats and refined on later ones".
   Shorter alternative: "two in a bulbous bow that later boats did away with".
   Keep "it slowed the boats in rough seas". You could add: "and threw up a bow wave that made periscope-depth work harder".
2. **Pressure at 300 ft (11).** Replace "about 130 lb on every square inch" with: "about 133 lb on every square inch, on top of the air's own 14.7".
   Alternative: "about 133 lb per square inch more than at the surface".
   Leave the readout constant (1.4585 psi/m, gauge) unchanged.
3. **Thermocline (12).** Replace "much of the ping is bent away and scattered" with: "much of the ping is bent away, downward and past it". Then: "Below it lies a shadow zone the sound barely reaches."
   Replace "captains dived for the layer on purpose" with: "submarine captains dived under the layer on purpose".
4. **Oerlikon / radar (7b, 7d).** If the page describes the boat as built (pre-war), add a mid-war qualifier, for example: "as refitted mid-war, with an Oerlikon aft of the bridge and a radar mast".
5. **Layer depth (13).** Wherever the 180 ft layer is shown to readers, add: "(the layer's depth changes with season and sea; 180 ft here is illustrative)". Remove the unsourced "14 to 180 m" from the comment, or mark it as indicative.
6. **Speed factor (14).** Optional: "about 36× slower than life" is closer to 1500/42, but "about 35×" is acceptable.
7. **Net cutter and compartment layout (7e, 7f).** These are unverified. Keep them only if they are drawn from a specific plan or photograph, or phrase the layout as "broadly" or "approximately".
