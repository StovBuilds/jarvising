ENIGMA: a teardown in scroll

Entry 001 on jarvising.com: a scroll-driven teardown of the Enigma I on a night watch in Hut 6 at Bletchley Park, from oak lid to reflector, ending in a faithful machine you can type on. It is a WebGL scene built with Three.js and TypeScript, and it takes about two minutes.

You scroll, and the machine comes apart. Nine chapters run from the hero orbit through a timeline of Scherbius's patent. The camera reads the card inside the lid, the oak case lifts into six labelled layers, the middle rotor slides offstage and explodes into its own stack, one keypress is traced as glowing current through plug board, rotors and reflector and back, and the rotors step like an odometer. Two chapters tell the story of the people who broke it, from Rejewski's paper reconstruction in 1932 and the Pyry hand-over of July 1939 to Turing and Welchman's bombes.

The last beat is a working Enigma I: rotors I, II and III, reflector B, ten plug pairs, the double-step anomaly included. Type on your keyboard or tap the keys; the lamps answer and a paper tape keeps both alphabets. "Copy secret link" bundles the start position and ciphertext into a URL, and because Enigma is reciprocal, whoever opens it watches the machine type the plaintext back out. Sound is opt-in and synthesised live.

The written entry, with what it does, how it was made, how to make your own and its sources, is at jarvising.com/projects/enigma.
